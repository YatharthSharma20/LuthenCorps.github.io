Checking Load Calculations for a Residential Renovation

Marcus WebbStructural EngineerConstruction8 years experience

Loading video…

The Problem

A homeowner wanted to remove a load-bearing wall to create an open-plan kitchen. The contractor needed structural drawings and an engineer's stamp before the city would issue a permit.

What Happened

Marcus walks through the process of evaluating whether a load-bearing wall can be removed, calculating the required beam size to replace it, and producing the stamped drawings the city requires for a building permit.

The Actual Work

  1. Visited the site and measured the existing structure — wall dimensions, span, floor-to-ceiling height.
  2. Reviewed the original building plans from the city's records office. They were from 1978 and partially illegible.
  3. Identified the load path: the wall was carrying roof loads and a portion of the second-floor joists.
  4. Calculated the tributary area and determined the total load the replacement beam would need to carry.
  5. Sized the beam using the National Design Specification for Wood Construction tables — ended up specifying a laminated veneer lumber (LVL) beam.
  6. Checked the bearing points at each end of the beam. One end landed on the foundation. The other needed a new post carried down to a footing.
  7. Drew up the structural details in AutoCAD and wrote the engineering letter.
  8. Reviewed the final package, stamped the drawings, and sent them to the contractor for permit submission.

Result

Permit was approved on first submission. The contractor installed the beam and the renovation was completed without structural issues.
