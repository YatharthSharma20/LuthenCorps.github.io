The Actual Work

  1. Started the workday around 7:30 AM by listing the most important tasks across several active substation projects.
  2. Reviewed his own designs and peer-reviewed other engineers' work, checking for errors that could cause costly equipment failures or safety issues.
  3. Worked on protection and controls for substations, ensuring breakers, relays, current transformers, and associated wiring would respond correctly during faults.
  4. Joined project coordination calls with field teams, project managers, and other groups involved in permitting, environmental work, construction, and engineering.
  5. Reviewed substation drawing packages, moving from high-level one-line diagrams to three-line diagrams showing CT connections and then detailed wiring diagrams used by field crews.
  6. Checked a 230 kV circuit-breaker tripping scheme, comparing the wiring drawings against the elementary diagrams to verify that everything matched.
  7. Used MicroStation to inspect and modify the engineering drawings, searching for equipment and checking connections digitally.
  8. Discussed project tasks and updates with coworkers throughout the day and coordinated work across multiple active projects.
  9. Finished the day by reviewing emails, recording notes for the next day's work, and wrapping up around 5:30 PM.

Conclusion

Completed the engineering reviews and verified that the substation protection and control designs, wiring diagrams, and circuit-breaker tripping schemes matched. Left notes for the next day's work and wrapped up around 5:30 PM.
